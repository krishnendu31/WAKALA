## Script

### Slide 1 — Title · 0:00–0:15
> Good morning. We are WAKALA — Arabic for “mandate”. Autonomous AI, accountable actions. We answer Challenge 27: securing the AI agents that will run government services.

### Slide 2 — The challenge · 0:15–0:35
> The challenge describes half of government services moving to agentic AI within two years. Today an agent usually holds a broad role or a reusable credential. So when an agent is wrong or manipulated, nobody can prove who acted, what it was allowed to do — or stop it without stopping the whole service.

*Source: the figure is from the Challenge #27 profile (Cyber Security Council). It is the organiser's framing, not our statistic.*

### Slide 3 — How it works · 0:35–1:05
> WAKALA puts an independent gateway between every agent and every protected service. Each agent signs every request. Each request is checked against a mandate bound to one citizen's case: tool, amount, recipient, expiry. When agents delegate, WAKALA issues a narrower signed child mandate — authority can never grow. Behaviour Watch flags misuse, officers intervene, and every decision is hash-chained. It is platform-neutral: the control sits at the service, not inside the model.

### Slide 4 — Live prototype · 1:05–1:40
> This is the real prototype. Mariam asks to renew her residency permit. The delegated Insurance Agent tries to read another citizen's record. WAKALA blocks it before it runs — no data returned. Security Operations sees the same case: the violated rule, the verified chain, and only that one mandate suspended. An officer reassigns the task, and Mariam's renewal completes. An unrelated case never stopped.

*Point at panels 1 → 2 → 3 as you speak.*

### Slide 5 — Evidence · 1:40–2:10
> We measured it. On 1,200 labelled synthetic requests, full WAKALA executed zero of 720 unauthorized operations and denied zero of 480 legitimate ones; a deliberately weak baseline executed 624. In the Attack Lab, seven of eight attacks succeed against the baseline, none against WAKALA. We also found and fixed a recovery flaw: 2 of 20 became 20 of 20, five times over. These are synthetic benchmarks, not government statistics.

### Slide 6 — Business model · 2:10–2:30
> Our customers are entities deploying agents, the central digital-government and cyber authorities, and platform suppliers who must prove equivalent protection. We propose an annual entity licence tiered by services and agents, a supplier conformance kit, and integration services.

### Slide 7 — 90-day pilot · 2:30–2:45
> The 90-day pilot starts in Shadow Mode on one low-risk service — observe only. Then we enforce one case type with officers in the loop, and finish with recovery drills and a go/no-go on measured results.

### Slide 8 — Status and ask · 2:45–3:00
> This is a working prototype on synthetic data — not production, and no live model yet. We ask for one pilot entity and a sandbox. WAKALA: autonomous AI, accountable actions. Thank you.

## Delivery notes

- **One speaker or two.** With two, switch at slide 4 (the demo) so the second speaker owns the prototype and evidence.
- **Say "synthetic" out loud** on slide 5. Judges will trust the numbers more, not less.
- **Do not demo live inside the 3 minutes.** The slide 4 screenshots are genuine captures. Keep the live prototype open in a second window for the Q&A (see `05_Demo_Runbook.md`).
- **If time is cut short**, skip slide 6 and say: "Business model and pilot plan are in our submission pack."
